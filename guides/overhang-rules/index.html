Overhang rules by state

Freight that hangs past the trailer counts toward the legal length once it passes the state's allowance, and it must be flagged. Here is each state's front and rear overhang limit.

Updated September 2026 · Figures from FreightDeck's audit of 51 US jurisdictions and its monthly market-rate check

The limits

Rear overhang allowances cluster at 4 ft (29 states), 6 ft (5 states), 15 ft (5 states). Front overhang is usually 3 to 4 ft. 50 of 50 states require flags by day and lights at night on any overhang.

Why it matters

A 45 ft beam on a 48 ft flatbed is legal. The same beam on a 40 ft hot shot has 5 ft hanging off the back, which is over the rear allowance in every state and makes the whole combination over-length: a permit, and in some states an escort. Questions

How much overhang is legal on a flatbed?

Typically 4 ft rear and 3 ft front before it counts toward length; a few states allow more. Beyond that the load is over-length and needs a permit.

Does overhang need a flag?

Yes in 50 states: a red or orange flag by day and a red light at night on the end of the overhang.
