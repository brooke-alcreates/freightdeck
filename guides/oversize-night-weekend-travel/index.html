When can an oversize load move?

Travel windows set the pace of a move more than distance does. A load that could run 600 miles in a day on the interstate runs 300 when the state stops it at sunset. Here are the rules.

Updated September 2026 · Figures from FreightDeck's audit of 51 US jurisdictions and its monthly market-rate check

Daylight, night and weekends

34 of 51 jurisdictions are daylight-only for routine oversize loads, typically half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset. 15 allow night movement in defined conditions, usually for loads under a set width with extra lighting: Arizona, Arkansas, California, Delaware, Iowa, Kentucky, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, North Carolina, North Dakota, Tennessee, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin. 8 restrict weekend travel: Alabama, Florida, Indiana, Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island.

What it does to the schedule

Oversize loads average about 300 miles in a daylight day once escorts, fuel and the permit's routing are counted. So a 900-mile daylight-only move is three days, three days of escort day minimums, and two overnights per car. The same move through night-permitted states can be two. When a route can be chosen, the states' travel windows are worth as much as the mileage.

Questions

Can oversize loads travel at night?

In 15 states, yes, under conditions (usually width limits and extra lighting). 34 states are daylight-only for routine loads.

Can you haul oversize on Sunday?

In most states, yes, within the same dimensional limits. 8 states restrict weekend travel: Alabama, Florida, Indiana, Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island.
