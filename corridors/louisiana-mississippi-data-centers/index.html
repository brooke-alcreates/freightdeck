Data center corridor · Richland Parish, Louisiana and Madison County, Mississippi

Heavy haul into Louisiana and Mississippi data centers

Multi-gigawatt campuses in northeast Louisiana and central Mississippi are among the largest single projects in the country. Equipment lands at the Port of New Orleans or Port of Houston and moves on I-20 and I-55.

Updated September 2026 · Rules verified against each state's source · Route states: Louisiana, Mississippi, Texas, Arkansas

What moves, and why it is all oversize

A hyperscale campus takes dozens of loads that do not fit a standard trailer: substation transformers at 100,000 to 400,000 lbs, backup generators at 12 to 14 ft high on the skid, switchgear and power modules that are legal-weight but over-width, and chillers and cooling towers that are wide and tall. Every one of them needs a permit in each state it crosses, and most need pilot cars. The permit is the small part; the escorts, route surveys and the specialized truck are where the cost sits.

Planning notes for Louisiana and Mississippi

  • Start with the heaviest piece. The transformer decides the trailer, the axle count and whether the move is a superload. Everything else on the campus is routine oversize by comparison.
  • Permit lead time is schedule, not paperwork. Superloads take one to three weeks per state for engineering review. Build it into the delivery plan before the equipment leaves the factory or the port.
  • Daylight-only states set the day count. Oversize loads move about 300 miles a day in daylight. Every extra day is another day of escort minimums.
  • Cross-check the last mile. County roads, overhead lines and site entrances near new campuses are the usual failure point. A route survey is cheap next to a stuck transformer.
  • Quote every truck, not the average. Twelve loads on one job can span four trailer types and three permit classes. FreightDeck's Truck Builder assigns each piece to the cheapest legal trailer and prices each truck.

Questions

What size transformer needs a permit into Louisiana and Mississippi?

Any load over 8 ft 6 in wide, over 13 ft 6 in high (the lowest legal height on this route, Louisiana), or over 80,000 lbs gross needs a permit in every state it crosses. A utility-scale transformer is over on all three.

How many pilot cars does a data center transformer need?

Usually one to two, plus a pole car for tall loads. The strictest width trigger on this route is over 12 ft in Louisiana. Above about 16 ft wide or 200,000 lbs most states treat it as a superload with police escorts and a route survey.

How far in advance should we apply?

Routine permits issue in one to three business days per state. Superloads take one to three weeks because an engineer reviews the route and every bridge. Plan the permit lead time into the equipment delivery schedule, not after the transformer ships.
